The work

My approach

If any line reads wrong, the channel above is the fix.

  • The guide is the sorting principle Your words: "I help people understand places, make connections, solve problems, and move forward." Every image that survives the edit answers to that: a man who knows these places and helps people through them.
  • You, at home in it, and the care you take The throughline is a man comfortable in his own territory, and the care you put into readying a house before it meets the market. Not posed ease, the real thing, caught while you work.
  • The work is already scheduled, so we photograph the schedule Painters at work in a Highland Park listing, a condo going live in a vintage Evanston building, a family house reaching the market. Nothing staged; the day supplies the truth.
  • The Skokie house gets the time it deserves A place that holds your history, likely going to market the day we shoot. Which of those frames ever becomes public stays your call alone, later.
  • Editorial profile, not real estate marketing Your register, kept: experienced, approachable, curious, confident, local. Polished where a purpose demands it, warm and in motion everywhere else. The interviewer side of your work gets found on location, never staged.
  • Your name on the sign, a brand that is yours alone One frame in front of a sign carrying your name. You work solo, and these images brand you as an individual, distinct, intentional about location, context, and personality. That intention is the difference, and it is rare in this field.
  • Built for years, not posts Clean headshots by purpose, verticals, text-room horizontals, banner widths. The formats you named arrive as categories, so the library keeps serving bios, sponsor kits, and press long after July.
